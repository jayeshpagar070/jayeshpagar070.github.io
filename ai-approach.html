Traditional vs AI-Driven

Traditional work moves through people and hand-offs: someone reads, someone interprets, someone writes it up, and knowledge stays in heads. My approach gives each step an AI partner with clear rules and a written output – and keeps a person on every decision that needs judgement.

ActivityTraditional wayMy AI-driven way
Finding business gaps Workshops and opinions; gaps surface after something breaks AI compares what a process or system should do with what it actually does, and lists every gap with evidence and a priority
Requirements analysis Read, interpret, and hope nothing was assumed AI extracts goals, rules, states and acceptance criteria – and tags every claim as verified, [NOT VERIFIED] or [GAP]
Learning a new domain Weeks of shadowing and scattered knowledge transfer AI-guided, read-only walkthroughs produce a glossary, a process map and a reviewed handover in about a day
Ideas & concepts A few options from whoever is in the room AI generates and stress-tests many options quickly; I shortlist against cost, risk and the business mission
Goals, vision & roadmap Long documents that drift from reality Goals tied to measurable outcomes and a ranked, ordered roadmap – with open decisions listed separately for leadership
Execution of repetitive work People repeat the same manual steps every cycle Specialised agents do the repeatable parts unattended; people focus on exceptions and decisions
Reporting & decisions Copy-paste into spreadsheets; numbers that can't be traced Reports generated from machine-readable results – every number traces back to its source
Knowledge Lives in individuals; leaves when they leave Captured in reusable prompts, rules and documentation, so the next person or session starts with it

My Method

Seven stages that work for any business problem – a process, a product, a team workflow or a compliance requirement. Each stage pairs what AI does fast with what I decide.

01

Discover gaps & inefficiencies

AI: maps the current process, compares it with what it should be, and surfaces delays, duplicate effort and manual hand-offs.

Me: validate with the people who do the work and decide which gaps matter most.

02

Analyse requirements

AI: turns stories, specs, tickets and recordings into goals, rules, states and acceptance criteria.

Me: keep unproven assumptions out – anything not verified is tagged, not promoted to a rule.

03

Generate concepts

AI: drafts several solution concepts, from a small process fix to full automation, with trade-offs.

Me: shortlist what is practical, affordable and maintainable for the team that will own it.

04

Define goals & vision

AI: turns the chosen concept into measurable goals, milestones and a ranked roadmap.

Me: agree success metrics with stakeholders before anything is built.

05

Align with the mission

AI: checks each goal against business priorities, compliance rules and constraints.

Me: escalate product and business decisions to the people who own them.

06

Automate & build

AI: specialised agents build, run and repair the repeatable parts, and produce evidence for every result.

Me: own the design, the guardrails and every irreversible action.

07

Measure & improve

AI: reports outcomes against the agreed metrics and spots where the next gain is.

Me: feed lessons back as written rules, so the same mistake never repeats.

And the loop starts again

Each cycle starts from better rules, better data and a clearer view of where the next inefficiency is.

Responsible by Design

Speed is only valuable if the results can be trusted. These rules are part of how I work, not an afterthought.

AI tags its claims

Every statement is marked as verified, not verified, or a known gap – so assumptions never become requirements.

Humans own irreversible actions

Signing, cancelling, money-moving and product-rule decisions stay with people. AI prepares the evidence; a person makes the call.

Never hide a problem to look good

Automation may fix how a check runs, but is never allowed to weaken what it checks. A real problem stays visible until it is fixed.

Encode the rules, not just the steps

Lessons learned are written into persistent rules, so the same mistake doesn't repeat – for the next session or the next person.
